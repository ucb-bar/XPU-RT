# ROS control-loop jitter, measured on the K1

Real ROS 2 Jazzy C++ nodes running the real ModelBlaster kernels, with an independent 100 Hz
control timer alongside the perception chain. Source: `scratchpad/ros_ctrl_starve.cpp`,
built and run on the board against `yolo/nav/ctrl` object files generated by ModelBlaster.

| runtime | control timer | median gap | p95 | max | worst-case rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ROS 2, SingleThreadedExecutor | asks 100 Hz | 10.02 ms | 59.36 ms | **74.70 ms** | **13 Hz** |
| ROS 2, MultiThreadedExecutor | asks 100 Hz | 10.03 ms | 59.30 ms | **62.10 ms** | **16 Hz** |
| XPU-RT (w4 board trace, 12 instances) | scheduled 10 ms | 4.87 ms | 6.56 ms | **12.41 ms** | **81 Hz** |
| XPU-RT (b4 board trace, 12 instances) | scheduled 10 ms | 5.86 ms | 7.54 ms | **13.04 ms** | **77 Hz** |

## What this settles

`mlp_control` is 0.08 ms of work and has its own core in the static partition, so on paper it
is never the bottleneck, and its MEDIAN rate confirms that: 10.02 ms, 99.8 Hz, exactly what
its timer asked for.

Its TAIL does not. A `SingleThreadedExecutor` serves every callback on the node graph from one
thread, so while the ~31 ms perception callback is executing the control timer cannot fire.
The result is a control loop that is nominally 100 Hz and is starved to 13 Hz in the worst
case. A real-time stability claim rests on the worst case, not the median.

The `MultiThreadedExecutor` does not fix it (59.30 ms p95). That is the configuration a
careful ROS engineer reaches for, which is why it was measured: the failure is not an artifact
of choosing the naive executor.

XPU-RT bounds the same quantity by construction -- `mlp_control` is placed inside a guaranteed
10 ms window every period -- and the board traces confirm it: worst observed gap 12.41 and
13.04 ms across two independent runs.

## Why it matters for the flight comparison

Earlier pairs flew the two arms at their chain latency, which is perception FRESHNESS. That
was the wrong quantity: freshness differs by only 5.6 vs 8.4 cm of travel per update at cruise
speed, and the flight outcome barely separates. What separates them is control-loop jitter,
and the measured worst-case gaps (12.41 ms vs 74.70 ms) are a 6x difference.
